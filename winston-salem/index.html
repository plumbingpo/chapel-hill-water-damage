What Usually Goes Wrong in Winston-Salem Homes

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around Winston-Salem, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Even a slow leak matters, because wood and drywall that stay damp will rot and grow mold.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. Tree roots, grease buildup and heavy rain overloading the public system are the common triggers. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and Winston-Salem properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: Cooling equipment produces a steady stream of condensation whenever it runs in damp weather. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. In Winston-Salem we often find this only after a stain or sagging drywall gives it away, so a yearly drain check helps.

Preventing Water Damage in Winston-Salem Homes

Most of the water damage we see in Winston-Salem homes started with something small that gave warning first. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Begin at the hoses. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Clean gutters and downspouts that discharge well away from the house keep rain out of the foundation.

Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure everyone in the house can turn it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. If you find something in your Winston-Salem home that worries you, such as a stain that keeps growing, have it looked at before it becomes a cleanup.
